# Verified References — Technical Completion Audit

Audit date: 19 Aug 2026.

These are the references that actually matter for the technical framing. The list is intentionally small.

## Foundational input-output

1. **Leontief, W. W. (1936).** “Quantitative Input and Output Relations in the Economic Systems of the United States.” *The Review of Economics and Statistics*, 18(3), 105–125. DOI: `10.2307/1927837`.
   - Role: foundational quantity input-output formulation.
   - Verification: JSTOR volume record lists the title, author, pages and DOI.
   - https://www.jstor.org/stable/1927837

2. **Ghosh, A. (1958).** “Input-Output Approach in an Allocation System.” *Economica*, 25(97), 58–64. DOI: `10.2307/2550694`.
   - Role: historically important allocation/supply-side IO reference.
   - Important: this project does **not** use the Ghosh quantity model.
   - https://www.jstor.org/stable/2550694

## Inoperability terminology

3. **Haimes, Y. Y., & Jiang, P. (2001).** “Leontief-Based Model of Risk in Complex Interconnected Infrastructures.” *Journal of Infrastructure Systems*, 7(1), 1–12. DOI: `10.1061/(ASCE)1076-0342(2001)7:1(1)`.
   - Role: early Leontief-based infrastructure inoperability/risk framing.
   - https://ascelibrary.org/doi/10.1061/%28ASCE%291076-0342%282001%297%3A1%281%29

4. **Haimes, Y. Y., Horowitz, B. M., Lambert, J. H., Santos, J. R., Lian, C., & Crowther, K. G. (2005).** “Inoperability Input-Output Model for Interdependent Infrastructure Sectors. I: Theory and Methodology.” *Journal of Infrastructure Systems*, 11(2), 67–79. DOI: `10.1061/(ASCE)1076-0342(2005)11:2(67)`.
   - Role: formal IIM terminology if the manuscript retains “IIM-style inoperability.”
   - https://ascelibrary.org/doi/10.1061/%28ASCE%291076-0342%282005%2911%3A2%2867%29

## Capacity constraints, rationing, disruption and IO optimization

5. **Hallegatte, S. (2008).** “An Adaptive Regional Input-Output Model and its Application to the Assessment of the Economic Cost of Katrina.” *Risk Analysis*, 28(3), 779–799. DOI: `10.1111/j.1539-6924.2008.01046.x`.
   - Role: adaptive IO with capacity/rationing mechanisms following disasters.
   - https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1539-6924.2008.01046.x

6. **He, P., Ng, T. S., & Su, B. (2015).** “Energy Import Resilience with Input-Output Linear Programming Models.” *Energy Economics*, 50, 215–226. DOI: `10.1016/j.eneco.2015.05.007`.
   - Role: direct precedent for IO + linear programming in energy resilience.
   - https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eneco.2015.05.007

7. **He, P., Ng, T. S., & Su, B. (2019).** “Energy-Economic Resilience with Multi-Region Input-Output Linear Programming Models.” *Energy Economics*, 84, 104569. DOI: `10.1016/j.eneco.2019.104569`.
   - Role: close precedent for capacity/disruption constraints and final-demand-deficit outcomes in an IO-LP framework.
   - https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eneco.2019.104569

8. **Oosterhaven, J., & Bouwmeester, M. C. (2016).** “A New Approach to Modeling the Impact of Disruptive Events.” *Journal of Regional Science*, 56(4), 583–595. DOI: `10.1111/jors.12262`.
   - Role: alternative optimization-based post-disruption allocation methodology.
   - https://doi.org/10.1111/jors.12262

9. **Pichler, A., & Farmer, J. D. (2022).** “Simultaneous Supply and Demand Constraints in Input–Output Networks: The Case of Covid-19 in Germany, Italy, and Spain.” *Economic Systems Research*, 34(3), 273–293. DOI: `10.1080/09535314.2021.1926934`.
   - Role: especially important closest conceptual precedent. The paper explicitly shows that outcomes depend strongly on rationing assumptions and uses optimization to characterize best-case feasible allocations.
   - https://doi.org/10.1080/09535314.2021.1926934

10. **Yagi, M., Kagawa, S., Managi, S., Fujii, H., & Guan, D. (2020).** “Supply Constraint from Earthquakes in Japan in Input–Output Analysis.” *Risk Analysis*, 40(9), 1811–1830. DOI: `10.1111/risa.13525`.
    - Role: supply-constraint IO literature and critique of naïve supply-side quantity formulations.
    - https://doi.org/10.1111/risa.13525
