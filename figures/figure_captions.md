# Final Figure Captions

## Figure 1
**Coverage behavior under a D35 capacity restriction.** Variant A follows the analytically proven path `z_D35 = 1-d/d*` until `d*=0.22544` while all non-D35 final-demand coverage remains at one. Variant B is shown only at the three frozen severities; the D35 household floor remains binding and construction absorbs the principal equal-sector additive adjustment until its analytically derived exhaustion point `d=0.14740`. No continuous Variant-B path is implied by the plotted points.

## Figure 2
**Additive versus lexicographic max-min on the same household-floor feasible set.** Solid lines report worst non-D35 coverage; dashed/dotted lines report output-weighted loss as a fraction (OWL/100). Max-min substantially raises the worst-covered sector but increases output-weighted loss relative to the additive rule. The max-output reference demonstrates that neither coverage rule minimizes output loss.

## Figure 3
**Sector-level concentration under the equal-sector additive household-floor rule.** Non-D35 sector coverage is ranked from lowest to highest at each frozen severity. At d=.05 and .10 only construction is materially below full coverage; at d=.20 construction is exhausted and L68B becomes the next absorber. The concentration follows the analytical loss-leverage ordering of the transformed additive problem.

## Figure 4
**Local parameter robustness versus structural convention sensitivity.** The left group shows the fraction of Monte Carlo trials in which construction remains the unique interior non-D35 sector at household-floor d=.10. The right group shows the maximum absolute sector-level coverage difference between equal-sector and monetary weighting for residual Variant A and household-floor Variant B. The two experiments are intentionally separated rather than connected by a line: stability around one weight vector does not imply stability to a qualitatively different weighting convention.
